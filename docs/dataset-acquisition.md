## Decision and collection protocol

Prioritize metadata/protocol files, not the entire audio archives. Check exact release, license, commercial/display restrictions, SHA256 and speaker/recording/prompt/generator fields. Build an overlap graph across IndicSynth–IndicSUPERB–Kathbath, IndicVoices–IndicVoices-R, ASVspoof–PartialSpoof, and LJSpeech–WaveFake–FoR. Preserve source recording, original speaker, target voice, transcript and all derived segments in one split. Unknown lineage is a quarantine flag, not permission to random-split. Keep one permitted generator family out of both training and validation for untouched held-out-family testing. The existing pretrained detector’s original training membership remains an unresolved overlap limitation.

The team has now ruled out private or team-recorded audio for this milestone. Acquire only public datasets whose exact source and derivative rights permit this research and presentation use. Public availability is not itself a license grant. For Hinglish/Indian English, resolve the upstream recording, speaker, transcript and generator lineage before assigning any split; quarantine clips without it. Do not create synthetic derivatives of public voices unless their source license and generator terms expressly permit that use. The earlier consented team-recording route is no longer in scope.

The new public candidates above improve language or metadata coverage but do not jointly provide cleared rights, speaker/source ancestry, both labels and an isolated generator for Hindi, Indian English, Hinglish and Marathi/Punjabi. No audio or gated files were downloaded, no terms were accepted, and D02 remains blocked. The smallest authorized next step is a permitted, public, source-linked Hinglish/Indian-English route; then inventory a small metadata sample before downloading audio.

A further September 27 card review rejected IndicCMix as a spoken code-switching shortcut and downgraded the large Hinglish aggregate to an unverified-class inventory. The [Indic-CodecFake paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2159/) identifies IndicSUPERB as its genuine source, as does IndicSynth; any future codec-fake candidate must be included in the same cross-release overlap graph, not treated as independent because it uses a different generator. Its project repository did not supply an auditable license, metadata release or acquisition route in this review. These findings do not unblock D02.

A later public-only search added MLAAD v9 as a noncommercial synthetic-only candidate and rejected DeepFox 2.0's email-gated route. Indic-FUSION offers explicit Hindi–Marathi source/synthetic pairing but descends from gated IndicVoices-R and has no independent derivative-rights clearance. None supplies a legally and lineage-cleared Hinglish plus Indian-English genuine/synthetic pair; no files or terms were acquired.

September 27 local-synthesis route check: [Indic Parler-TTS](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts) claims Apache-2.0 weights and Hindi, Marathi and English support, but its official files require contact-sharing acceptance, Punjabi is unofficial, and Hinglish output quality/rights have not been established. The public [IndicF5-Hinglish fine-tune](https://huggingface.co/Saravananravi/indicf5-hinglish) requires reference audio and was trained on OpenSLR-104; do not use OpenSLR-104 as an independent held-out source against that generator, or clone a voice without separately cleared reference rights. Neither model was acquired or run. A locally generated synthetic set remains a proposal, not a cleared second class or held-out generator.

September 27 IndicSynth metadata check: the public Hugging Face API reported ungated revision `c0a10386b723717aff682f757bd67f72983f269f` with 1,414 repository entries and Hindi Parquet shards; no shard was downloaded. One viewer metadata row each from Hindi, Marathi and Punjabi exposed `Generative Model=freevc24`, source/target speaker IDs and source/target reference-audio filenames, but no separate original-recording ID or transcript for those sampled VC rows. The rows endpoint serves the live release, so these three examples are not a revision-pinned sample. They confirm that *some* derivative-grouping fields exist, not that the full release can be safely split or used in the final test. Before audio acquisition, pin the revision, audit source filenames against IndicSUPERB/Kathbath and confirm noncommercial presentation use; do not pair these with supposed independent genuine clips by file order.

One public OpenSLR-104 test archive was later acquired for a genuine-only exploratory check; see the [source/lineage and detector receipt](validation/d02-openslr104-2026-09-27.md). It supplies no synthetic examples, and the 30 selected excerpts have not been individually verified as audible code-switching. D02 and the four-language acceptance gate remain blocked. No gated model/dataset terms were accepted and no local audio or generated result is tracked by Git.

The [IndicSynth synthetic-only probe](validation/d02-indicsynth-2026-09-27.md) then scored 12 fixed, revision-checked public viewer rows across Hindi, Marathi and Punjabi entirely in memory. Six of nine scorable clips triggered HIGH; three scorable synthetic clips were only ELEVATED, while three other clips were rejected/insufficient. A single temporary public WAV used to inspect format was removed. This does not create a source-disjoint two-class corpus: XTTS source fields are absent in sampled rows, IndicSUPERB ancestry is only partly traceable, Indian English/Hinglish are absent, and the noncommercial academic license does not authorize redistributed demo audio. The report JSON is Git-ignored; no threshold, model or app policy changed.
