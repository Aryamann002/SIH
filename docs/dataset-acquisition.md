## Decision and collection protocol

Prioritize metadata/protocol files, not the entire audio archives. Check exact release, license, commercial/display restrictions, SHA256 and speaker/recording/prompt/generator fields. Build an overlap graph across IndicSynth–IndicSUPERB–Kathbath, IndicVoices–IndicVoices-R, ASVspoof–PartialSpoof, and LJSpeech–WaveFake–FoR. Preserve source recording, original speaker, target voice, transcript and all derived segments in one split. Unknown lineage is a quarantine flag, not permission to random-split. Keep one permitted generator family out of both training and validation for untouched held-out-family testing. The existing pretrained detector’s original training membership remains an unresolved overlap limitation.

For Hinglish, recruit consenting adults on a separate team protocol: record natural Hindi/English code switching and prompted utterances locally with multiple speakers, microphones and rooms; assign opaque IDs, keep a signed consent ledger outside the repository, allow withdrawal, and separately opt in to research, voice synthesis and public demonstration. Make prompts original; retain source/segment linkage and split speakers before segmentation or augmentation. Synthetic Hinglish may use a locally run, properly licensed TTS voice that permits the intended use, or a clone of a speaker who expressly consented to synthesis. Record model/voice and prompt provenance. Never upload these recordings to Jev or another external service without explicit permission. Do not present a tiny team sample as population or fairness validation.
