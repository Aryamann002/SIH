# OpenSLR-104 Hindi–English genuine-only probe — 2026-09-27

This is exploratory development evidence, not the untouched VigilVoice test or a four-language accuracy result. The [official OpenSLR-104 release](https://www.openslr.org/104/) labels the Hindi–English speech as code-switched spoken tutorials and declares CC BY-SA 4.0. Only its 443 MB ASR test archive was acquired from `https://www.openslr.org/resources/104/Hindi-English_test.tar.gz`; no train archive, gated data or synthetic model was downloaded. The publisher did not provide a checksum on the inspected page. Local archive: 443,929,204 bytes, SHA256 `93e358b3bf8233a897fcd353c1f4f98fdda6b8c01b7eed17a70c7dd26e984b37`. It and the result JSON are ignored under `docs/validation/artifacts/datasets/` and must not be committed or bundled.

The archive contains 30 original mono 16 kHz PCM16 WAVs and Kaldi `segments`, `text`, `wav.scp`, `spkr_list`, `spk2utt` and `utt2spk` files. The 3,136 segment IDs join to 30 source recordings and 30 speaker IDs, exactly one original recording per speaker; metadata segment durations sum to 5.178 hours. 2,514 segments are at least four seconds long. This proves declared within-archive grouping, not identity independence from other corpora or the detector's unknown pretraining. The corpus is described as code-switched, but the selected excerpts have not been manually checked for audible code-mixing.

`scripts/probe_openslr104.py` verifies the pinned archive SHA256, joins segment/speaker metadata, selects the earliest eligible segment by source/start/ID for each speaker without looking at scores, crops exactly four seconds in memory, checks the WAV format/bounds, and calls the existing `evaluate_wav` upload path. No audio derivative is saved. The selected 30 clip hashes, source/utterance/speaker IDs, risk states, scores and timings are in ignored `docs/validation/artifacts/datasets/openslr104-genuine-probe-2026-09-27.json` (SHA256 `f4a8aa905b7af8bbd08e8f583979351e3b78cf9ab3522152b5a3d209884b641f`). Scorable coverage was **25/30**; HIGH false blocks among scorable genuine excerpts were **5/25 = 20%** (descriptive 95% Wilson interval **8.9%–39.1%**). This exceeds the frozen ≤10% observed validation bound on this small exploratory genuine-only sample, but it does not select a new threshold. The five unscorable clips stay in the denominator for coverage and are not silently called safe. All 30 selected sources, speakers and clip hashes are distinct. In-memory processing latency across all 30 excerpts had median **1.473 s** and nearest-rank p95 **5.810 s** on the warm Windows host; this is not microphone capture-to-alert time or a resource minimum.

The four clipping rejections were checked against their original selected PCM excerpts. Their peak absolute sample values were 32743, 32768, 32767 and 32768; respectively 2, 23, 11 and 39 samples met the current near-full-scale (at least 0.99) trigger, including 0, 9, 4 and 27 full-scale samples. The quality gate is therefore responding to near-full-scale signal rather than a metadata-only error. This does **not** establish that all four are audibly distorted or that the gate is optimally calibrated; no clipping rule was relaxed on these clips.

The selected segment transcripts were inspected without changing selection: 24/30 contain both Latin and Devanagari characters. This is transcript-level script mixing, **not** a verified spoken Hinglish label for the exact four-second prefix. Many earliest segments are tutorial greetings, so the fixed first-segment rule may overrepresent introductions; the 30 clips are not a random or balanced speech-content sample. Do not pick replacement segments based on these scores.

Reproduce with the public archive placed at the path below:

```powershell
python -m pytest -q -p no:cacheprovider tests/test_probe_openslr104.py
python -m scripts.probe_openslr104 docs/validation/artifacts/datasets/Hindi-English_test.tar.gz --output docs/validation/artifacts/datasets/openslr104-genuine-probe-2026-09-27.json
```
